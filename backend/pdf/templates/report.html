Method & provenance

How the fit score is built
{{ club }}'s recruitment profile lists the traits it wants for the position. Each trait is scored from a per-90 percentile within the player's position cohort, then weighted by evidence tier — a directly measured metric counts fully, a correlated stand-in at 0.7. Demonstrated fit divides the total by the club's full requirement, so a trait that could not be measured counts as unmet. Measurable fit divides only by what was measured, which is why it runs higher and is not used for ranking.
Confidence
Minutes played and the club profile's own data quality, multiplied by the proportion of the requirement that could be measured. Coverage is a multiplier rather than one term among several, so playing time cannot substitute for having measured what the club asked for.

How wages are estimated
A wage band is modelled from the player's Transfermarkt valuation, which tracks pay far more closely than a league or minutes tier does — wage rises with value but sub-linearly, so the curve is calibrated on published Premier League figures and scaled down for the other four leagues. The band is ±25%, because reported wages are ranges and press figures often fold in bonuses. Where no valuation exists the estimate falls back to league, minutes and age, which is materially less reliable and labelled as such.
Sources
Player performance: FBref, {{ season }} Big-5 leagues, from a cache built offline. Market value and contract expiry: Transfermarkt, dated per player on the candidate pages. Wages: contract data where available, otherwise modelled from league, minutes and age and labelled est. Club tactical profile and required traits: internal knowledge base.
Limits
Percentiles are relative to the position cohort in the stated league and season, not to all of football. Market values are third-party estimates, not asking prices. A modelled wage is a band, not a contract figure. No candidate already at {{ club }} is included, and availability is only as good as the contract date shown.
